\begin{frame}{Context}
  This is an account of the SciFi detector's slow control system: what got built, end to end, ahead of the data taking at PSI this October.
  \bigskip
  \begin{columns}[onlytextwidth,T]
    \begin{column}{0.53\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{itemize}
        \item The old slow control page, \texttt{Scifi-monitor.js}, grew organically over the years into something that worked -- but was \textbf{\textcolor{ETHRed!70!black}{hard to trust}} and even harder to extend.
        \item So it was \textbf{replaced end to end}: a proper set of \textbf{\textcolor{metacolor!65!black}{Python scripts}} driving the detector, and a new \textbf{\textcolor{uicolor!65!black}{custom operations page}} a shifter can actually rely on.
        \item What follows is the complete picture -- \textbf{\textcolor{instcolor!65!black}{installation}}, the \textbf{\textcolor{uicolor!65!black}{interface}} itself, \textbf{\textcolor{confcolor!55!black}{configuration}}, \textbf{\textcolor{calcolor!55!black}{calibration}}, and how results get \textbf{\textcolor{savecolor!45!black}{saved}}.

\begin{frame}{From hardware to shifter}
  \begin{columns}[onlytextwidth,T]
    \begin{column}{0.56\textwidth}
      \begin{itemize}\small\setlength{\itemsep}{6pt}
        \item The chain runs both ways: \textbf{hardware status} flows up to the shifter, and the \textbf{\textcolor{uicolor!65!black}{shifter's changes}} flow back down to hardware, though not always instantly -- some changes only apply at specific trigger points (e.g.\ a run start).

\begin{frame}{The SciFi detector, briefly}
  \begin{columns}[onlytextwidth,T]
    \begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
      \footnotesize
      \begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
        \item \textbf{2 rings}, US / DS -- 12 modules each; \textbf{24 SMB} (12+12) merge into 12 super-modules via a passive DAB.
        \item \textbf{12 FEB} (1:1 with LV); \textbf{96 ASIC} (32 ch.\ each) $=$ \textbf{\textcolor{pwrcolor!65!black}{3072 channels}}; \textbf{24 HV channels} (1:1 with SMB).

        \item SMB and DAB connect via microtwisted-pair links, carrying the raw \textbf{MuTRiG3} data downstream.

  \small The first version of the acceptance logic assumed a locked PLL would show up as a clear peak in the timing histogram -- a reasonable-sounding guess. \end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Calibration entry points -- a deliberate simplification}
  \texttt{thr\_run.py} / \texttt{vbr\_run.py} / \texttt{pll\_run.py} bypass SciFi's own \texttt{CalibrationFactory} (never wired to a real entry point) and instead:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item build \texttt{Detector} + \texttt{Calibration} \textbf{directly}
    \item open their \textbf{own} \texttt{MidasClient}, with \texttt{try/finally: disconnect()}
  \end{itemize}
  \bigskip
  \begin{block}{Judged safer than pixel's own pattern}
    Pixel passes PySequencer's \texttt{seq} object in as the client -- never independently verified. SciFi's explicit connect/disconnect is a stronger guarantee.
  \end{block}
\end{frame}
